\subsection{Extended Domain Generalizability Study}\label{sec:evaluation:extended_domains}

To test whether the empirical formulation findings hold beyond the six core IPC domains, we extended the evaluation to three additional pure STRIPS planning benchmarks featuring distinct topological and operational structures:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Parking11}}: Complex vehicular rearrangement requiring cars to be parked and shuffled on and off curbs. Like \texttt{Blocksworld}, it exhibits recursive state dependencies and clear spatial stacking constraints across 4 action schemas.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Visitall}}: Pure typed spatial navigation where an agent must visit a set of interconnected grid locations across a single action schema (\texttt{move}).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Driverlog02-bfs}}: Heterogeneous multi-agent logistics extending classical transportation by modeling drivers who must navigate paths, board trucks, and transport goods across 6 action schemas.
\end{enumerate}

All three extended domains were evaluated under the identical protocol ($R = 3$ independent runs, $k_{\max} = 3$ repair iterations, penalty $P = 6$).

The extended domain study confirms three important generalizability principles:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Generalization Across Diverse Problem Archetypes}: The closed-loop AST agent achieved 100\% bidirectional semantic success on all three extended domains in a single iteration ($1.00$ mean iterations, $3/3$ successful runs for every domain). This establishes that the agent's generation and validation pipeline is strictly domain-agnostic and functions reliably across diverse problem topologies, from single-operator grid searches (\texttt{visitall}) to complex multi-agent coordination (\texttt{driverlog}) and spatial stacking structures (\texttt{parking11}).
  \item \textbf{Elimination of Zero-Shot Brittleness}: On \texttt{parking11}, unguided single-turn prompting (\texttt{ZERO\_SHOT}) suffered connection drops and inconsistent precondition grounding across repeated runs (failing 1 out of 3 runs, yielding a penalized mean of 2.67 iterations). In contrast, the closed-loop agent maintained invariant convergence in $1.00$ iteration across all runs.
  \item \textbf{Syntactic Robustness Invariant}: As observed in the core benchmark, Syntactic Validity Rate (SVR) remained 100.0\% across all three conditions and domains, confirming the reliability of AST parsing across diverse PDDL schemas.
\end{enumerate}
